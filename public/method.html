    How the ranking works.

    Two numbers, both transparent. The risk score orders everything by how likely it is to hurt you now. The priority turns that into a deadline you can defend in an audit.

    Risk score, 0 to 100

    SignalPointsWhy
    Exploited in the wild (CISA KEV, or Microsoft reports exploitation)50Confirmed attacks matter more than any theoretical score.
    Used in ransomware campaigns15The outcome is usually severe and fast.
    EPSS probability of exploitation in 30 daysup to 20Linear up to 50%: predicts attacks before they are confirmed.
    CVSS base scoreup to 10Technical severity, one point per CVSS point.
    Public exploit code (Metasploit module, Nuclei template or proof of concept on GitHub)10Ready-made code lowers the skill needed to attack.
    Recent activityup to 5Full within 14 days of release or KEV listing, fading to zero at 60 days.

    Tiers: Patch now from 80, High from 60, Notable from 40, Track below 40. The score is capped at 100. Exploitation confirmed in the last 30 days is always Patch now, whatever the score. An update's score is the highest score among the CVEs it fixes, plus a small boost when it fixes ten or more.

    Priority and patch-by date

    P1Exploited in the wild, in CISA KEV, Critical with CVSS ≥ 9, or EPSS ≥ 50%7 days
    P2Critical, publicly disclosed, public exploit code, EPSS ≥ 10%, or Microsoft assesses exploitation as more likely14 days
    P3Important30 days
    P4Everything else90 days

    The window starts at the Microsoft release. When CISA sets an earlier deadline for a KEV entry, that deadline applies.
